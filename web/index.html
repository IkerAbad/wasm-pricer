What the Greeks mean

plain English

A call is the right to buy at the strike, the agreed price, and a put is the right to sell at that price. Both are European, so they can only be exercised at expiry. A positive Greek means the option value rises when that market input rises. A negative one means it falls.

Delta

How much the option value changes when the stock rises by 1.00. Calls have positive delta and gain when the stock rises, while puts have negative delta and gain when it falls.

Gamma

How much delta changes when the stock rises by 1.00. It is positive when you own a call or put, so the position becomes more sensitive to the stock as it moves away from the strike.

Vega

Vega measures how sensitive the option value is to volatility, the size of the stock's swings. It is positive when you own a call or put, so more volatility makes the option more valuable.

Theta

How much value the option loses as one year of calendar time passes. It is usually negative when you own an option, because time value shrinks as expiry approaches.

Rho

Rho measures how sensitive the option value is to interest rates. Calls usually have positive rho and puts usually have negative rho, so higher rates help calls and hurt puts.

Vanna

How much delta changes when volatility rises. The sign tells you whether the option becomes more or less sensitive to the stock as volatility changes.

Volga

How much vega changes when volatility rises. It is usually positive when you own an option, so its sensitivity to volatility grows as volatility grows.

Charm

How much delta changes as one year of calendar time passes. The sign tells you whether the option becomes more or less sensitive to the stock as expiry gets closer.
